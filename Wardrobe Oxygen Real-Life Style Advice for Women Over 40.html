What to Wear to a Music Festival Over 40

I've attended a lot of music festivals over the years, and if there's one thing I've learned, it's that the best festival outfit isn't necessarily the one that looks coolest in a photo. It's the one that still feels good after hours of walking, standing, sitting, dancing, sun, wind, dust, and whatever else the day throws at you.

And no, being over 40 doesn't mean you need a special category of “age-appropriate” festival fashion. You don't suddenly have to trade personality for practicality. But by this point in life, many of us know ourselves well enough to realize that painful shoes, an annoying bag, and an outfit that needs constant adjusting can ruin an otherwise fantastic day.

The best music festival outfits for women over 40 combine comfortable footwear, breathable clothes, useful layers, sun and weather protection, and a bag that lets you keep your hands free. From cowboy boots and denim to dresses, sneakers, bold prints, and practical rain gear, the goal is to dress like yourself while preparing for a very long day.

I've put together five very different music festival outfits below, along with what I've learned about dressing for festivals after years of attending them.

Going to a concert instead?

A music festival has different wardrobe considerations from a concert in an arena, club, or theater. If you're seeing one artist rather than spending the day at a festival, visit my What to Wear to a Concert Over 40 guide instead.

What Should a Woman Over 40 Wear to a Music Festival?

My best advice is to shop your closet. Be yourself, and wear what you know and trust.

You don't need to go out and buy fringe, sequins, and boho skirts galore. While these sorts of looks may get the most engagement on social media, you will see that the people having the most fun are those who dressed for the day, not the camera.

A good festival outfit usually has a few things in common:

  • Shoes you can walk and stand in for hours. This is not the time to break in a new pair. There is no wrong style of shoe if it's comfortable, supportive, and can keep up with the elements. I've done tall boots, hiking boots, combat boots, and sneakers.
  • Clothing that doesn't require constant adjustment. A bra that doesn't hike up or stab you in the armpit. Pants that don't stretch out and need to be hiked up all day. Socks that won't slide down. Shorts that won't ride up into your crotch. Skirts and pants that won't fall into mud and porta-potty puddles.
  • A hands-free bag. This is very dependent on your festival; some don't allow backpacks, some require clear bags, and almost all of them have size requirements. Whatever the regulations, have it a bag that won't dig into your shoulder, hold what you need, and let you also hold someone's hand, a beverage, or use that porta-potty.
  • Sun protection. Even if it's cloudy, even if it's October, you want sunscreen and the ability to reapply it. If there's a chance of sun, bring a hat and sunglasses. I also recommend a scarf or bandana to mop your brow, wet and cool your neck, or cover your decollete or scalp.
  • A layer for changing weather. Even a sweltering summer day can get chilly when the sun goes down.
  • A little personality. Do not think that because you are a certain age, size, or whatever you can't have fun with your fashion. You will see, the coolest people are the ones who aren't taking life, or their outfits, too seriously. Wear the t-shirt of your favorite band, add face glitter, choose color and prints. You'll be glad you did.

Some music festivals are one-day events, some span almost a week. Some take place in a remote field in the middle of nowhere and you're sleeping in a tent, some take place in a city and you head back to your home or a hotel at the end of the day. What you wear is also dependent on the genre of music and the weather/time of year. Check the weather, if you're traveling pack an extra outfit and an extra pair of shoes, and always put function over fashion.

5 Music Festival Outfit Ideas for Women Over 40

As I have gotten older, I have come to really appreciate the urban music festivals where I am able to recover from the day in a climate-controlled space with a proper bed and a hot shower. The outfits below are more geared towards those sorts of music festivals, versus say Bonnaroo, which I attended for many years and required specific attire for the situation. But I tried to encompass a range of music genres and personal style aesthetics with garments and accessories that are great for us grown women.

1. A Modern Country Music Festival Outfit

The Formula: tank + midi skirt + button-front shirt + comfortable boots + straw hat + hands-free clear bag + bandana + sunglasses

There is no need to dress up in cowboy boots and a 10-gallon hat to go to a music festival featuring country, bluegrass, or folk music. However, this outfit not only fits the vibe, but has a ton of practicality, too.

The denim skirt is loose and midi-length, so you can easily bend down, sit on the grass, and use the facilities with ease. Add modesty and chafe protection with a pair of slip shorts underneath. Tie the shirt around your waist in the heat, and put it on when the temps drop (or you want some sun protection). Denim can take a beating and hides dirt and stains well, so it's a smart choice for a music festival.

The bandana isn't just decorative. Tie it around your neck or hair, use it as a headband, or keep it in your bag for dust and sweat. A hat is not only stylish, but smart when out in the sun. It will keep your head cooler and protect you from being burned.

As for cowboy boots, wear them only if they're boots you already know you can walk in for hours. Your festival ticket is not the occasion to discover where your new boots rub.

2. A Colorful Music Festival Outfit

The Formula: loose cotton pants + high-neck tank + packable rain/windbreaker + waterproof boots + clear belt bag + sunglasses + trucker hat + lipgloss with SPF

If you love color and print, don't tone yourself down because you're over 40. A music festival is a stellar place to rock a pair of party pants, play with patterns, color, and texture. Just be sure you keep it comfortable and durable.

Loose cotton or lightweight pants can also be a great alternative to shorts when you want sun protection or don't want your bare legs touching every surface you encounter during the day. Have them run a bit short (or tuck them into the boots) so they don't soak up any grossness. A lace-up boot is sleek enough to work with a cropped pant; I own an older version of these boots and have loved them for music festivals, travel, and rainy days.

3. A Music Festival Outfit With a Dress

The Formula: dress in breathable fabric + button-front shirt + boots + packable hat + clear bag + aviator sunglasses + bandana

A dress can be one of the easiest things to wear to a festival because it's essentially an entire outfit in one piece.

Look for something that allows you to walk freely, sit comfortably, and move without constantly pulling it up or down. Natural or breathable fabrics are especially nice in hot weather. If thigh chafing is an issue, I highly recommend a pair of Thigh Society shorts.

Again, I added a denim shirt because it's a bit heavier than poplin, but still lightweight enough to shove in a bag or tie around your waist. Denim also can get a bit grubby without ruining your look.

4. A Comfortable Rock or Alternative Festival Outfit

The Formula: t-shirt sharing your favorite band or venue + longer denim shorts + overshirt + supportive sneakers + bandana + sunglasses + trucker cap showing your personality + hands-free hydration or backpack

This is the music festival outfit I likely wear the most. And this is the outfit you are likely going to see the most worn by folks of all ages at all genres of music festivals.

A music festival is the perfect time to nerd out. Love live music? Have a favorite venue? Are you a musician? Seen the headliner a dozen times before? Been to previous years of this festival? Wear your heart on your sleeve, your chest, your head, and with buttons on your bag.

A pair of longer shorts prevents chafing and is more comfortable when sitting on the ground. A flannel is a nod to the grunge days, but also very on trend again, and a great way to protect your bum on the ground or add warmth when the sun goes down. And I own these exact sneakers in a different color; I took out the insoles and added my own and have worn them to many fests and live shows with plenty of comfort.

5. A Music Festival Outfit for Unpredictable Weather

The Formula: jeans + button-front shirt + sweater + packable rain shell + weather-friendly boots + simple necklace + aviators + clear bag

Not every festival takes place in blazing summer heat.

This article is inspired by a music festival at the end of September here in Maryland that is taking place on a weekend that is looking to be cold, breezy, with occasional rain showers. I was texting with a friend as to what she planned to wear, and envisioned this look for her.

The button-front shirt can be tucked in, or left out. Unbutton an extra button to give movement and a more festive feel. Add a camisole underneath for a bit of warmth and modesty, but a necklace that hangs in the neckline makes the look purposeful. The jeans are loose, but not terribly long so they won't drag in any mud. The work boots can handle all sorts of terrain and weather.

Knowing this festival is one where you can head back to your car or hotel during the day, I added both a sweater and a rain jacket. The sweater (which I own and love) can be tied around the shoulders or waist. The jacket is packable and long enough to keep you dry without being too bulky. It didn't make it into the collage, but I also would recommend taking a baseball cap you can clip to the bag; it can help in a drizzle and also keep the hood of your jacket from blocking your vision.

What Shoes Are Best for a Music Festival?

If you know me, you know I will never suggest you to sacrifice your feet for an outfit.

At a music festival you may walk miles without realizing it, stand for long stretches, dance, navigate uneven terrain, and then walk all the way back to your car, hotel, shuttle, or campsite. Your shoes will be the most important thing you're wearing.

My favorite options are supportive sneakers and well-broken-in boots. Depending on the weather and terrain, a comfortable hiking shoe or weather-resistant boot may make more sense than either. There may be broken glass, quicksand-like mud, spilled beer, and even vomit. Keep the sandals and Crocs at home or just for padding around your campsite.

Whatever you choose, test it before the festival. Wear the shoes for a full day, not just around your house, and see how your feet feel. Try out socks; you may find a synthetic is better at preventing blisters, or a merino option may keep you from being sweaty and cold.

I would avoid shoes that:

  • Are brand new
  • Rub anywhere before you even leave home
  • Have smooth soles (get some grip)
  • Require you to grip them with your toes (I'm looking at you, flip-flops and Birkenstocks)
  • Aren't secure enough for crowds or uneven ground
  • You would be devastated to have covered in dust or mud

And if you use orthotics or insoles in everyday life, a music festival is certainly not the day to leave them home.

What Kind of Bag Should You Carry to a Music Festival?

The bag policy at music festivals has changed drastically in recent years. It is important, even if you have been to this fest before, to check the website for what they are now allowing.

Some allow hydration packs, but only a certain size with limited pockets. Some permit smaller purses and belt bags, but they need to also have limited pockets. A lot of festival have banned backpacks. Many have just changed to the same policy as stadiums only allowing clear bags of a certain size.

I recommend a bag with a wide strap to better disperse weight, one that is hands-free, and the largest size you are permitted to bring so you can carry everything you're bringing and anything you may purchase and maybe a bottle of water and maybe something for the friend who doesn't have room in their bag.

How to Dress for Heat, Rain, and Changing Temperatures

Festival weather has a way of making you experience several seasons in a single day.

For a Hot Music Festival

I recommend either go activewear, or going loose and breathable. A pair of linen or gauze pants and matching shirt with a tank can be more comfortable than a little sundress. Activewear designed for wicking moisture can be a good choice if you are comfortable wearing it, but still have some sort of layer for sun protection.

For a Rainy Music Festival

Even if it's just spitting rain, you're going to feel cold and wet and miserable pretty quickly. Umbrellas are rarely allowed, so bring a lightweight or packable rain jacket with a hood. A baseball cap will handle soggy hair and keep rain and your hood from falling in your eyes.

But beyond that, think about the entire outfit getting wet. Shoes should handle mud. Pants shouldn't drag on the ground. Your bag should protect anything that can't get wet. If you are able to go back to your car or hotel, consider packing a change of clothes so you can feel less soggy for the evening.

For a Festival That Gets Cold at Night

A hot afternoon doesn't mean a hot evening. Instead of carrying one bulky jacket all day, look for thin layers that can work together: a button-front shirt, lightweight sweater, and packable jacket can give you far more flexibility.

Music Festival Style Mistakes I've Learned to Avoid

I've been to a dozen different music festivals over the decades. I've been a size 10, I've been a size 16, I've even been pregnant. They've been in the rain, in the blistering heat, in the mountains, the desert, the beach, and the city. And these are the lessons I have learned and want to share with my fellow grown women:

Don't wear brand-new shoes. Even the most comfortable-looking shoes can reveal a pressure point after several hours. This even includes the flip-flops or Crocs at your campsite and the insoles in your sneakers. Everything should have been pre-tested.

Don't bring your entire life. That bag gets heavier every hour. Pack for the single day, or the single visit to the festival grounds for that day. You don't need a whole pack of sanitizing wipes, just a few in a Ziploc bag. You don't need multiple protein bars, you only need one lip product… keep things simple and small.

Don't dress only for the afternoon forecast. Look at the temperature when you'll actually be leaving the festival. Especially if you've been sweating, it's going to get chilly when the temps drop.

Don't wear anything that requires babysitting. If you're constantly pulling, tucking, tying, adjusting, or worrying about flashing someone when you sit down, choose something else.

Don't wear anything too precious. Festivals can involve dust, spilled drinks, grass, mud, sunscreen, sweat, and other people's feet. Assume everything you bring will get stained, torn, or muddy.

Don't plan an outfit only for the photo. You can spot a festival newbie a mile away by their complicated Instagram-worthy outfit. A music festival is about the music, the people, and the moment. Wear what lets you be in that moment and if pictures are taken, they'll capture that joyful vibe.

My Festival Outfit Test

Before I wear Something to a Music Festival, I ask:

  • Can I walk in it?
  • Can I stand in it?
  • Can I sit on the ground in it?
  • Can I handle the expected weather in it?
  • Can I wear it for 8+ hours without fussing with it?
  • Do I feel like myself in it?

If the answer is yes to all six, it's probably a good festival outfit.

What Should You Not Wear to a Music Festival Over 40?

You should not wear anything that prevents you from enjoying the reason you bought the ticket in the first place.

You should not wear uncomfortable shoes, bags that dig into your shoulders, or clothes that make it difficult to dance, lounge on the ground, or pee.

You should not wear anything that makes you uncomfortable. If you are most comfortable in an oversized t-shirt and leggings, or a sequined tube top and tutu, then that is what you should wear. That being said, a music festival is a perfect time to try out a crop top, a bold color, a fun pair of sunglasses, or wacky knee-high compression socks.

Personal style doesn't expire, and a music festival is a particularly good place to remember that.

Music Festival Outfit FAQs

What should a 40-year-old woman wear to a music festival?

Wear comfortable clothes that reflect your personal style and work for the weather and terrain. A good festival outfit combines supportive shoes, breathable clothing, a hands-free bag, sun protection, and layers you can add or remove throughout the day. Being over 40 doesn't require dressing more conservatively for a music festival.

What shoes are best for a music festival?

Supportive sneakers and broken-in boots are two of the best choices for music festivals. Consider how much you'll walk and stand, the terrain, and whether rain or mud is expected. Avoid wearing brand-new shoes to a festival.

Can you wear jeans to a music festival?

Absolutely. Jeans can be a practical choice for cooler weather, evening festivals, or events where you want more coverage. In hot weather, choose lightweight denim or consider a denim skirt, shorts, or looser pants instead.

What should I wear to a music festival if it might rain?

Wear layers topped with a lightweight waterproof or water-resistant jacket and shoes that can handle wet or muddy ground. Avoid long hems that may drag through mud and check whether the festival permits ponchos or other rain gear.

What should I wear to a country music festival over 40?

Denim, comfortable cowboy boots, a tank or tee, a lightweight button-front shirt, and a sun hat make an easy country festival outfit. There's no need to dress in a Western costume unless that's genuinely your style; choose elements that already work with your wardrobe.

The Best Music Festival Outfit Is the One You Can Forget About

The best thing you can wear to a music festival is something that lets you walk, dance, sit, eat, sweat, get caught in the weather, and stay out far later than you planned while still feeling like yourself.

At this stage in life, I don't think getting dressed should be about proving that we can still wear something or deciding that we're too old to wear something. It's about knowing ourselves well enough to choose what lets us have the best experience.

Wear the comfortable shoes. Bring the layer. Protect yourself from the sun.

And then go enjoy the music.
