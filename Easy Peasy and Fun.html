How to Play Hangman Pen and Paper Game: Rules and Better Alternatives

If you are looking for a classic, screen-free pen and paper game that builds vocabulary, sparks quick thinking, and keeps kids entertained for hours, Hangman is an absolute winner, kind of. All you need to get started is a pencil, a piece of paper, and a couple of players ready to test their word skills.

While generations of players grew up sketching gallows without a second thought, the traditional imagery has become a bit too grim for many modern classrooms, family game nights, and international households. And for a good reason. It may seem innocent in some parts of the world (and I am using the word “innocent” very loosely here, since the game still depicts an inappropriate scene), but in others it carries a heavy burden of history no one can be proud of.

But at its core, the game is a very good one for ESL!

The good news? You don’t have to sacrifice the thrilling, high-stakes suspense that makes the game so fun. In this guide, we’ll cover the classic rules of Hangman, hand you a free printable template, and introduce you to exciting, danger-filled Hangman alternatives perfect for the classroom, like Alien Abduction, that keep the adrenaline pumping without the dark history.

How to Play the Hangman Pen and Paper Game

You don’t need much to get a game going, just two players (or a group), a piece of paper, and a pen or pencil. No matter what version of Hangman you play, the rules are the same. Guess the word before the drawing is complete in order to win!

One player is the Game Master, and the other is the Guesser.

Here is how a round works:

Choose a Secret Word: The Game Master thinks of a secret word or phrase. They draw a series of blank horizontal lines on the paper, where each line represents one letter in the word.

Example: For the word APPLE, you draw 5 dashes: _ _ _ _ _

Take Turns Guessing Letters: The Guesser suggests a letter of the alphabet.

Check the Letter: If the letter is in the word, the Game Master writes it in the correct blank space(s). If the letter is not in the word, the Game Master writes the incorrect letter off to the side (so the Guesser remembers not to pick it again) and begins drawing the hangman piece by piece.

Win or Lose: The Guesser wins if they correctly guess all the letters before the drawing is completed. The Game Master wins if the drawing is finished before the word is solved.

How many Moves in Hangman?

The standard classic Hangman game usually gives the Guesser 6 to 8 incorrect moves (or “strikes”) before the game is lost, depending on how detailed the drawing is.

There is no set rule that determines how many moves the game should have, so you can adjust it to have more or fewer guesses.

Why Hangman Is Being Reconsidered in Modern Classrooms and Homes

While generations of players grew up sketching gallows without a second thought, the game is increasingly viewed as inappropriate by modern educators and parents for a few key reasons:

Morbid Imagery: Simulating an execution via a gallows is inherently dark or violent.

Historical and Cultural Sensitivity: In many parts of the world, the visual carries a heavy burden tied to real-world capital punishment and historical lynchings, making it deeply distressing or insensitive for certain players.

Normalized Violence: There’s also a point to be made about imagery like this normalizing violence.

Safer, lighter, and more creative alternatives offer the same fun without the insensitive connotation the original game carries.

Kid-Friendly Hangman Alternatives

Part of what made the Hangman game so popular is the fact that no matter your drawing skills, the drawing part is not challenging. A few lines and a stick figure, even with uneven lines, can easily be drawn by pretty much everyone.

And then there’s the imminent danger. The sense of urgency that the word puzzle needs to be solved before something bad happens.

I’ve tried my best to keep both those elements in mind when making these hangman alternatives. As well as the number of moves!

Monster

If you want a game that swaps out the gloominess of traditional hangman for pure, spooky excitement, Monster Behind the Door is your absolute best bet. Here is the setup: First, draw a simple rectangular door on your paper.

Inside that room lurks a creature waiting to burst out. Every time the guesser misses a letter, a new part of the terrifying monster gets sketched onto the page.

Will they crack the code, or will the beast fully materialize before time runs out? Eek!

Choose Your Challenge Level (How Many Moves)

The best part about this alternative is how easy it is to scale up or down depending on the difficulty of the word or the age of the players:

For a Quick 6-Move Game: Keep it fast-paced and high stakes! The first move draws the line for the body of the monster. Second is the head (no face). Then comes the pair of legs. Next, the first pair of monster arms. Then the second. The last one is the scary face details.

For an 8-Move Game: Before going into drawing the monster, start by drawing the doors – divide it into two moves.

More moves? Just divide the parts into more moves.

Alien Abduction

If you want a game that trades the gloominess of traditional hangman for out-of-this-world sci-fi excitement, Alien Abduction is your absolute best bet.

Here is the setup: First, draw a flying saucer hovering near the top of your paper, with a little stick figure standing helpless on the ground below.

Down comes the tractor beam! Every time the guesser misses a letter, a new part of the scene gets sketched onto the page, bringing the little guy closer and closer to a one-way trip to outer space.

Will they crack the code in time, or will the alien successfully pull off the heist? ZAP!

Choose Your Challenge Level (How Many Moves)

The best part about this alternative is how easy it is to scale up or down depending on the difficulty of the word or the age of the players:

For a Quick 6-Move Game: Keep it fast-paced and high stakes! Move 1: a little stick figure human. Step 2: The dome of the saucer. Move 3: the disc of the saucer. Step 4: Bottom part of the dome. Move 5: Alien flying the saucer. Move 6. Two beams.

For an 8-Move Game: Give the guesser a bit more breathing room by drawing the stick body in two moves and divide the beams into two moves as well!

More moves? Just divide the elements into more stages, or adding even more details.

Prisonman (Jailtime): Locked Up and Nowhere to Go!

If you want a game that trades the gloominess of traditional hangman for classic cartoon jailhouse suspense, Prisonman (or Jailtime) is your absolute best bet.

Will you crack the code before lockup is permanent, or is this stick figure doing hard time? Cuff ’em!

The best part about this alternative is how easy it is to scale up or down depending on the difficulty of the word or the age of the players:

For a Quick 6-Move Game: Keep it fast-paced and high stakes! Break the drawing down into 6 core parts:The stick figure’s head (with a sad face) The stick figure’s body and limbs. The top and bottom horizontal frame lines of the cell The first vertical prison bar The second vertical prison bar The final bars sealing the cell completely shut

For an 8-Move Game: Give the guesser a bit more breathing room by drawing the prison piece-by-piece.

More moves? Just add extra vertical bars or structural details like a brick wall border around the cell!

Burglar Will Steal Your Word

If you want a game that trades the gloominess of traditional hangman for pure, sneaky suspense, Burglar is your absolute best bet. This is the perfect “heist” themed challenge for players who love a little light-hearted cartoon danger.

Here is the setup: First, draw a simple stick-figure burglar wearing a mask. They are creeping toward a giant swag bag labeled with a big dollar sign ($).

Every time the guesser misses a letter, a new piece of the burglary gets sketched onto the page, moving the thief one step closer to pulling off the perfect crime. Will they crack the code, or will the Burglar make off with the loot? Stop that thief!

For a Quick 6-Move Game: Start with the line for the burglar’s body (1). Next, draw the head with the mask on (2). Draw a pair of arms (3). Next, draw the legs (4). Draw the bag (5). Draw the dollar, or another currency, sign on the bag (6). Heist successful.

For an 8-Move Game: Draw the body line(1). Next, draw the head with the mask on (2). Draw one arm (3). Draw another arm (4). Next, draw one leg (5). Draw another leg (6). And then the bag (7). Draw the dollar, or another currency, sign on the bag (8). Heist successful.
