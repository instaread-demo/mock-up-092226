Tokyo Events This Week: Moon Art Night, Fall Fests, and Peace Day

September 20th, 2026 | Patrick Balfe
  • Home
  • Events
Written by Patrick Balfe

Tokyo events for Monday, September 21, to Sunday, September 27, 2026.

Listen to audio version of this article

Instaread

With the harvest moon on Friday, this week is all about moon gazing. Moon Art Night returns to Shimokitazawa with a few new additions, while shrines, gardens, and observatories around the city host their own lunar events. Also worth checking out this week are Noh performances in Shinjuku, the Tokyo International Comedy Fest, and traditional festivals and fireworks around the city.

Shinjuku Gyoen National Garden Mori no Takigi Noh

Traditional Japanese Noh theatre is a uniquely immersive art form that lovers of Japanese culture can’t miss. At Shinjuku Gyoen National Garden, you can enjoy the drama surrounded by nature.

Shinjuku Gyoen National Garden Mori no Takigi Noh

Sep 24, 2026
¥6,000 – ¥11,000
Shinjuku Gyoen National Garden
Read more

Roppongi Matsuri

The gods enshrined at Tenso Shrine — located between Tokyo Midtown and the National Art Center — go for a spin around the neighborhood in their fancy (little) portable shrines on the Sunday starting at 12:30 p.m.

Nezu Shrine Festival

Nezu Shrine Festival has been held since the days when Tokyo was still Edo, when it ranked among the city’s grandest. What sets 2026 apart is the shinkōsai, the grand procession of the shrine’s large mikoshi, which takes place only once every four years.

Nezu Shrine Festival

Sep 26–Sep 27, 2026
Free
Nezu Jinja Shrine
Read more

Ōkunitama Shrine Autumn Festival/Chestnut Festival

The Autumn Festival at Ōkunitama Jinja Shrine is also called the Chestnut Festival because — you guessed it — the region is known for its top-quality chestnuts. Apparently, they were presented to the Tokugawa Clan during the Edo period because they are that good.

Ōkunitama Shrine Autumn Festival/Chestnut Festival

Sep 27–Sep 28, 2026
Free
Ōkunitama Shrine
Read more

Fukagawa Jugoya (Moon-Viewing) Festival

To celebrate the autumn full moon, Tomioka Hachimangū Shrine will be hosting a tsukimi (moon-viewing party) as part of its annual Mid-Autumn Festival. The shrine grounds will be lit up, and performers — such as Japanese dancers and singers — will appear live on a special stage.

Fukagawa Jugoya (Moon-Viewing) Festival

Sep 23–Sep 27, 2026
Free
Tomioka Hachimangū Shrine
Read more

Mukōjima-Hyakkaen Garden Moon Viewing

Moon viewing at the historical Mukōjima-Hyakkaen Garden will include cultural performances such as koto playing, as well as tea ceremonies and lantern painting.

Mukōjima-Hyakkaen Garden Moon Viewing

Sep 24–Sep 26, 2026
At the door: ¥150
Mukōjima-Hyakkaen Garden
Read more

Shinagawa Shukuba Festival

The Shinagawa Shukuba Festival, also known as the Shinagawa Shukuba Matsuri, is a festival celebrating Shinagawa’s history as the first rest stop on the Tokaidō Road heading south.

Shinagawa Shukuba Festival 2026

Sep 26–Sep 27, 2026
Free
Read more

Ōhara Naked Festival

On the first day of the Ōhara Naked Festival, participants carry portable, shoulder-borne shrines (mikoshi) to the sea for a dunking — which goes a long way towards explaining the costumes.

Ōhara Naked Festival

Sep 23–Sep 24, 2026
Free
Ōhara Station
Read more

Moon Art Night Shimokitazawa

The hip neighborhood of Shimokitazawa will celebrate the start of autumn and the Japanese tradition of tsukimi (moon viewing) by holding an art festival on its streets. You can expect powerful installations, headlined by Luke Jerram’s gigantic Museum of the Moon at the Shimokita Senrogai Open Space.

Moon Art Night Shimokitazawa

Sep 18–Oct 4, 2026
Free
Shimokita Senrogai Open Space
Read more

Ikebukuro Fukuro Festival (Mikoshi)

Ikebukuro Fukuro Festival takes over the entire neighborhood of Ikebukuro across two weekends. The first weekend includes an opening ceremony as well as mikoshi (portable shoulder-borne shrines) parades, taiko drum shows, lion dances, and heaps more.

Ikebukuro Fukuro Festival (Mikoshi)

Sep 26–Sep 27, 2026
Free
Read more

Peace Day

Peace Day on September 21 is an outdoor festival at Yoyogi Park, packed with events and activities. You’ll hear guest speakers, enjoy live music, and check out unique art exhibits.

Tokyo National Museum – Free Admission Day

Tokyo National Museum’s regular exhibitions are free to the public on the third Monday of September, the Respect for the Aged Day national holiday. The museum exhibits around 600 works of art in fields such as painting, calligraphy, sculpture, and archaeology.

Tokyo National Museum - Free Admission Day

Sep 21, 2026
Free
Tokyo National Museum
Read more

Tokyo Game Show

Tokyo Game Show remains one of the top gaming shows in the world — and 2026 is its biggest outing yet. The 36th edition marks 30 years since the first show in 1996, and runs for a record five days under the theme “The Longest Five Days of Nonstop Play.”

Tokyo Game Show 2026

Sep 17–Sep 21, 2026
Advance sales: ¥3,000
Makuhari Messe
Read more

Fiesta Mexicana

Taking place near Aqua City shopping center in Odaiba, Fiesta Mexicana celebrates Mexican traditions and food culture. Around 20 stalls offer artisanal crafts and authentic Mexican food. In past years, we’ve spied tacos, churros, and churrasco, as well as drinks like tequila and horchata.

Fiesta Mexicana 2026

Sep 20–Sep 22, 2026
Free
Odaiba Marine Park
Read more

Odaiba Drone Show

One thousand drones fly over Tokyo Bay on eight nights in September, drawing a “Pixel Moon” and a run of game, anime, and film characters above Rainbow Bridge. Watching costs nothing.

ODAIBA DRONE SHOW 2026

Sep 4–Sep 22, 2026
Free
Read more

Candlelight Concert: The Nutcracker and More

A string quartet and two ballet performers will take you on a journey through The Nutcracker, plus some pieces from Swan Lake and other recognizable classics.

Starry Moon Viewing at Cosmo Planetarium Shibuya

Cosmo Planetarium Shibuya marks the harvest moon with a one-night run of themed screenings, most of which finish with a rooftop viewing session over the Shibuya skyline.

Starry Moon Viewing at Cosmo Planetarium Shibuya

Sep 25, 2026
¥500 – ¥1,500

Kita Fireworks Festival

Around 10,000 fireworks will be shot into the sky to popular music, elaborate lighting effects, and drones.

Kita Fireworks Festival

Sep 26, 2026
Free
Arakawa Akabane Park
Read more

Ōarai Marine Fireworks Festival

The Ōarai Marine Fireworks Festival is one of the newer fireworks on the annual calendar, but it doesn’t mess around when it comes to scale and extravagance. On top of that, this year will also see an aerial appearance from Whisky Papa, Japan’s top aerobatic team.

Ōarai Marine Fireworks Festival

Sep 26, 2026
¥4,500 – ¥600,000
Read more

Koidegawa Spider Lily Festival

This is an annual event held along a three-kilometer stretch of river in Fujisawa City, Chigasaki City, and Samukawa Town. While it might not exactly be a contender for the big spider lily festival in Saitama, it’s certainly a wholesome, low-key option.

Koidegawa Spider Lily Festival

Sep 26, 2026
Free
Read more

Namaste India

With the links between India and Japan getting stronger, this festival gets bigger and bigger each year. There will be a large number of different food and drink stalls, musical performances, lectures, and other displays.

Namaste India

Sep 26–Sep 27, 2026
Free
Yoyogi Park Events Square
Read more

Pirates of Tokyo Bay: English & Japanese Improv Comedy

For over a decade, The Pirates of Tokyo Bay have been creating hilarious improvised comedy scenes, stories, and songs based on cues from the audience. The monthly shows are frequented by a loyal and inclusive fanbase and are a great way to get involved in Tokyo’s improv and comedy community.

Pirates of Tokyo Bay: English & Japanese Improv Comedy

Sep 27, 2026
Advance sales: ¥2,500At the door: ¥2,500
Read more

Japan-Korea Exchange Festival

Held at the Korean Cultural Center in Japan, the Tokyo version of the festival will feature everything from Korean food products to tae kwon do displays, and traditional dances and music, including performances by K-pop artists.

Japan-Korea Exchange Festival

Sep 25–Sep 27, 2026
Free
Read more

Tokyo International Comedy Festival

TICF is East Asia’s largest stand-up comedy festival. The event brings together Japanese and international comedians performing across the city in all manner of intimate club performances, showcases, competitions, podcasts, and experimental comedy events.

Tokyo International Comedy Festival 2026

Sep 17–Sep 27, 2026
¥2,500 – ¥7,000
Read more

Harvest Moon and Corn Moon Viewing Ikebukuro

Sunshine 60 Observatory turns its 60th-floor deck into a moon-watching station for four nights, covering both the harvest moon and the full moon that arrives two days later.

Harvest Moon and Corn Moon Viewing at Sunshine 60 Observatory

Sep 24–Sep 27, 2026
¥500 – ¥900
